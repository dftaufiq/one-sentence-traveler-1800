# Variants — internal pairwise tournament

Judge = RULES.txt brief, criteria in order: (1) explains what it is + does,
(2) 1800-grounded words, (3) literally true, (4) no filler/list, (5) one memorable image.
All counts verified by script (see session). All variants ASCII-only.

## Tournament

- F1 beats F2 — market square explains commerce/talk but not what the internet IS
  (a network joining people); the road explains the connective nature (criterion 1).
- F1 beats F3 (narrow) — the window covers two uses (speak, read) but no network
  idea; a window is passive, the internet is active connection (criterion 1).
- F4 beats F1 — same single image (road of light) but covers knowledge (page arrives)
  AND two-way communication (answer comes back); F1 covers communication only.
- F4 beats F2, F3, F5, F6 — F5 has two images and no network; F6 overclaims
  ("every book and letter") and is broadcast, not two-way; F2/F3 as above.
- V1 retired: "every house on earth" is a literally-false totality claim (criterion 3).

## Winner: F4 (168 chars / 168 bytes)

> Picture the great libraries of the world joined by one road of light to your door, so the page you ask for arrives in a heartbeat and an answer comes back just as fast.

Why it wins:
- One image (road of light — literally true: fibre carries light), one sentence.
- Criterion 1: network (road joining libraries to your door) + knowledge (page you
  ask for arrives) + two-way communication (answer comes back) — the most complete
  grasp of what the internet is and does of any variant.
- Criterion 2: every word pre-1800 (picture, libraries, road, light, door, page,
  heartbeat, answer). The word "internet" itself is avoided entirely ("Picture…").
- Criterion 3: no totality overclaim — "the great libraries" is a picture, "the page
  you ask for" is conditional, the reply is something the reader sends/receives.
- Criterion 4: 168/200 chars, no list of features, no filler.
- Criterion 5: "road of light to your door" + "an answer comes back just as fast"
  is the memorable beat; "in a heartbeat" is a fresh speed image (field uses
  "in a blink" / "before your tea cools").
- Distinct from the nearest field entry (bnbf's "Imagine the greatest library… road
  to your door… arrives in a blink"): ours adds the two-way reply, drops the wordy
  "holding more letters and books than a lifetime of reading" filler, and uses a
  different speed image. Not byte-identical to any known entry.
